# Nagel, "Unraveling in guessing games: an experimental study" (American Economic Review 85(5), 1995)

## What it is
The classic "p-beauty contest" experiment: players pick a number from 0 to 100, and the winner is closest to p times the mean (p = 1/2, 2/3 or 4/3); the only Nash equilibrium for p < 1 is everyone choosing 0 (`guessing-games.md:25` (p. 2)). For `harder-rounds` D4, side games whose payoff depends on every team's choice. The copy is a JSTOR scan hosted on a Paris School of Economics course page.

## Main results
- **Finite depth of reasoning** (`guessing-games.md:41` (p. 3)): first-round choices cluster at one or two steps of iterated reasoning from the mean of 50, not at the equilibrium.
- **Over rounds** the median falls toward the equilibrium, but the modal depth of reasoning does not increase (`guessing-games.md:41` (p. 3)).

## Assumptions against the Arena
- Students in 1995, not teams with AI agents; an agent asked the textbook question may answer at equilibrium or at depth 1–2. **For D4** *(inferred)*: ask a few agents to find where the field will be, then play one step beyond it, as `harder-rounds` suggests.
