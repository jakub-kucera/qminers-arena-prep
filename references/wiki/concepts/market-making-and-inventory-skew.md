# Market making and inventory skew

## Summary
The baseline in almost every top bot is **take → clear → make** around a fair value: take what is better than fair, trade at fair to free room under the limit, then quote one tick inside the book. Whether to skew quotes against inventory is settled for a pegged product (P4 Osmium: no skew, which the theory predicts); for the others it is open.

## What the sources say
- **The pattern.** Linear Utility's `take_best_orders` / `clear_position_order` / `make_orders` (`prosperity2-linear-utility/round1/round_1_v6.py:46`, `prosperity2-linear-utility/round1/round_1_v6.py:114`, `prosperity2-linear-utility/round1/round_1_v6.py:250`) is the ancestor of `python/bots/template.py`. Frankfurt's Resin trader takes at `wall_mid ± 1`, flattens at fair, and overbids inside the wall with full remaining room (`prosperity3-frankfurt-hedgehogs/FrankfurtHedgehogs_polished.py:275-331`).
- **Clearing at zero EV** frees room for better trades: about +3% PnL (`prosperity2-linear-utility/README.md:96`).
- **Skew variants shipped:**
  - Baguette Fromage Osmium: reservation price `10000 - position/12`, i.e. a linear shift (`prosperity4-baguette-fromage/Strategies/Phase1.py:240`); their round 5 market maker uses `wall_mid - 0.4 × position` with quote size 5 and a pull-back beyond ±8 (`prosperity4-baguette-fromage/Strategies/Phase2_Final.py:1374`).
  - DTU's plain market maker: `skew = position / limit × 4` ticks (`prosperity4-dtu-quant-lab/round_5/algorithmic/trader.py:656`).
  - Frankfurt: none; full room on both sides, flatten at fair.
  - Alpha Search: skew plus a half spread of 4 for non-bot quotes (`prosperity4-alpha-search/README.md:54`).
  - Ryan Challman: inventory skew `kq` enabled only where it held up across days (`prosperity4-ryan-challman/README.md:71`).
- **What failed:** an Avellaneda–Stoikov skew on pegged Osmium cost DTU 16,593 in backtest (`prosperity4-dtu-quant-lab/README.md:91`, their claim); a layered buy ladder on Hydrogel lost ~21,000 (`prosperity4-ryan-challman/README.md:268`); on news-driven products passive quotes were picked off (`uchicago-trading-2026/README.md:113-121`).
- **Plain beat clever on the live day.** DTU's hybrid replaced its elaborate strategies by the plain wall-mid market maker on UV_VISOR, TRANSLATOR and OXYGEN_SHAKE after live evidence of +11.3k, +10.8k and +0.8k (`prosperity4-dtu-quant-lab/round_5/algorithmic/trader.py:560-562`). Baguette Fromage's round 5 backbone was "broad market making" on every product (`prosperity4-baguette-fromage/Strategies/Phase2_Final.py:540`).

- **Quoting design under a tight limit** (heyman7913): two passive layers (inner ±5, outer ±7 from fair) so the position limit does not leave the bot idle, and **25 units reserved for aggressive takes** regardless of passive fills; their biggest early mistake was filling the passive layer first and having no room when a clear mispricing appeared (`online-snapshots/heyman7913_imc-prosperity-4.README.md:146-162`). They blended a volume-weighted mid with a slow EMA and an imbalance term `0.30 × micro + 0.70 × EMA + 2.0 × imbalance − inventory penalty` and tested it with a Spearman correlation (`online-snapshots/heyman7913_imc-prosperity-4.README.md:195-233`).
- **How dealers control inventory** (Harris, [source](../sources/harris-trading-and-exchanges.md)): move *both* quotes against the position, change the *sizes* (to sell down: larger ask size, smaller bid size), price aggressively only on the side you want to trade, and take another trader's quote only when the position is urgent (Table 13-2, `literature_md/harris-trading-and-exchanges/harris-trading-and-exchanges.md:6642-6654` (p. 297–298)). Our template moves both quotes (`skew`) but not the sizes. Cartea et al.'s volume-optimising maker grows the size on the inventory-reducing side (`literature_md/algorithmic-and-hft/algorithmic-and-hft.md:5976-6032` (p. 273–276)).
- **What the theory says** ([optimal quoting models](optimal-quoting-models.md)): GLFT's and Guéant's closed forms are "fair ± half-spread − skew × inventory", with a skew linear in inventory and independent of time, which is our `skew`. The theory supplies the coefficient from the product's fair-value volatility and fill-rate curve, and says it should be near zero on a pegged product.
- **Avellaneda–Stoikov as run in practice** ([Hummingbot](../sources/hummingbot-avellaneda-stoikov.md)): reservation `s − q·γ·σ²·(T − t)` and spread `γσ²(T − t) + (2/γ)·ln(1 + γ/κ)`, the published paper's eqs. 29–30, with γ and κ **derived from the user's min and max spread**, not estimated from the book (`online-snapshots/hummingbot-as-deep-dive.md:110-136`). As written, the reservation shift then has the same size for every nonzero inventory *(inferred, see the source page)*. Compare with the constant-coefficient linear skews above.
- **Order-book imbalance as a quoting signal** (Cartea, Donnelly & Jaimungal 2018, [source page](../sources/order-book-signals.md); the whole family is on [book imbalance](book-imbalance-and-micro-price.md)): define `ρ = (Vᵇ − Vᵃ)/(Vᵇ + Vᵃ)` from the volumes at the best bid and ask (*checked against the PDF*: `literature_md/order-book-signals/order-book-signals.md:75-79` (p. 5, checked)). On eleven Nasdaq equities ρ predicts the sign of the next market order and the price change right after it, and putting ρ into a limit-order market-making control problem raised out-of-sample profit by cutting adverse selection (`literature_md/order-book-signals/order-book-signals.md:43` (p. 2)). Their motivating findings: 91.6–99.9% of market orders are filled by the volume at the best price alone, so deeper limit orders rarely fill, and a strategy that always posts at the touch **has negative expected earnings** from adverse selection (`literature_md/order-book-signals/order-book-signals.md:61-63` (p. 4), `literature_md/order-book-signals/order-book-signals.md:87` (p. 6)). Caution from the AlgoTrade analysis: the sign of an imbalance's predictive power **flips per instrument** (`algotrade2026-pitfa19/analysis/mm_analysis.md:172-197`).
- **Whether market making pays at all** ([Bouchaud et al.](../sources/trades-quotes-prices.md)): a market maker nets half the spread minus the long-run impact of the trades it meets.
  - Loose inventory control ("slow"): `E[s]/2 − R∞`. Back to flat after each trade ("fast"): `(E[s]/2)(1 − C(1)) − R(1)`, with `C(1)` the correlation of consecutive trade signs (eq. 17.13–17.15, `literature_md/trades-quotes-prices/trades-quotes-prices.md:6599-6609` (p. 345, checked)).
  - On NASDAQ, competition holds this at break-even, and simple market making with inventory control loses about 0.10 of the spread per trade (`literature_md/trades-quotes-prices/trades-quotes-prices.md:6619` (p. 346)). In the zero-intelligence Santa Fe model it is profitable (`literature_md/trades-quotes-prices/trades-quotes-prices.md:3098` (p. 166)).
  - **Measured on Prosperity 3 round 1** (`arena.diagnose`): both margins are 0.2–0.4 of the spread per unit. Kelp has a spread of 2.32, `R(50)` of 0.27 against the mid and about 0 against the wall mid, and `C(1)` of 0.13 ([source page](../sources/trades-quotes-prices.md), "Assumptions against the Arena"). Prosperity behaves like the zero-intelligence case.
  - **But the takers are one-sided on Kelp and Squid Ink:** 35% of the trades at the touch are buys (42% of the volume), and most of `C(1)` is that imbalance (0.3² = 0.09; 0.04 net of it; Resin's 0.09 is balanced). A two-sided quote is then hit on the bid more often: in `arena.sim`'s `KELP_FLOW` preset (`python/arena/sim.py:223`), which copies the flow, the template's passive buys outnumber its sells 1.37 to 1, as on P3 (1.28), and it leans long (`IDEAS.md`, "Replay against a market that reacts").
- **Mean reversion helps the maker, trends hurt it** (`literature_md/trades-quotes-prices/trades-quotes-prices.md:6500` (p. 341)). A resting order's average cost is `φ_exec·d·ρ`, with `ρ` the return autocorrelation (eq. 21.18, `literature_md/trades-quotes-prices/trades-quotes-prices.md:7820-7822` (p. 415, checked)). Prosperity's mids have strongly negative lag-1 autocorrelation.
- **Inventory control through size, not price:** quote `υ₀·max(1 + κψ_{t−1}ε, 0)` on each side, more on the side that reduces the position (eq. 17.4, `literature_md/trades-quotes-prices/trades-quotes-prices.md:6514` (p. 342, checked)). This is Harris's "change the sizes". The template's `size_skew` implements it; on Prosperity data it costs a little PnL, as `skew` does (`IDEAS.md`).
- **Skew on a pegged product, measured:** Osmium (P4 rounds 1–2, 6 days, 2026-10-13): `skew` 0 is best on every measure with `wall_mid` (113.8k; 113.3k at 1, 110.0k at 2, 93.4k at 8; 108.7k at 1 under `worse` fills), and a fixed 10,000 makes 99.2k (`uv run python -m arena.grid python/bots/template.py 1 2 --data p4 --set PARAMS.ASH_COATED_OSMIUM.fixed_fair=10000 --grid PARAMS.ASH_COATED_OSMIUM.fair=fixed,wall_mid --grid PARAMS.ASH_COATED_OSMIUM.skew=0,1,2,4,8 --modes all,worse`). So `skew` only costs there, in line with [Ahuja et al.](../sources/mean-reverting-reference-price.md) and GLFT, and against DTU's A–S skew.
- **The discrete version of skew** ([Guilbaud & Pham](../sources/limit-and-market-orders.md)): when long, improve only the ask and keep the bid at the best; past a threshold, cross. A one-tick `skew` in the template does about this *(inferred)*.
- **Where plain market making loses** ([Albers et al.](../sources/market-makers-dilemma.md)): on a one-tick, crowded market (the bitcoin perpetual), quoting at the best on both sides lost almost 60% in three days, and quoting with the imbalance lost too (`literature_md/market-makers-dilemma/market-makers-dilemma.md:412-416` (p. 22)). Prosperity is the opposite case, wide bot-made spreads where the maker earns about half the spread a unit (`arena.diagnose`), so the warning applies only if the Arena's spreads are one tick and contested.
- **Inventory control with limit orders only** ([Jerome et al.](../sources/scaled-beta-policies.md)): clearing with market orders is costly; skewing a volume profile over many levels, more near the best on the side that reduces inventory, controls inventory with limit orders alone (`literature_md/scaled-beta-policies/scaled-beta-policies.md:85` (p. 3)). The one-level version is `size_skew`.
- **A measured adverse-selection gate** ([Zhao & Linetsky](../sources/mm-adverse-selection-rl.md)): the book exhaustion rate, market-order volume per second over the size at the best, is argued to be proportional to the adverse-selection cost (`literature_md/mm-adverse-selection-rl/mm-adverse-selection-rl.md:41` (p. 2)); computable from `Tick.trades` and `Book`.

- **When quoting pays at all** ([Gao & Wang](../sources/market-making-latency.md)): on a large-tick asset, only while uninformed market orders hit the quotes often enough against the rate of price jumps (`literature_md/market-making-latency/market-making-latency.md:7` (p. 1)); a per-product check before giving a product `make`.

## Where they disagree
Theory against practice ([contradictions](../contradictions.md)): whether quoting at the touch loses to adverse selection (row 12), how much of the limit to use (row 14), and whether quoting inside the wall can be trapped (row 16).

Whether a skew helps on a pegged product: DTU says an A–S skew lost 16,593 and reverted to a fixed anchor; Baguette Fromage shipped a linear skew (γ = 1/12) on the same product. See [contradictions](../contradictions.md), row 1. Neither number has been reproduced here.

## What it means for our code
`python/bots/template.py` has `skew` (default 0.0, a linear shift of both quotes by up to `skew` ticks at the full limit: `python/bots/template.py:710`), `clear_edge`, `take_edge` and `make_edge`. That is the linear variant, off by default. Since 2026-10-13 `skew` works toward a `target` position instead of zero (`python/bots/template.py:710`). Since 2026-10-13 `size_skew` adds Bouchaud et al.'s eq. 17.4: each quote's size times `max(1 ∓ size_skew·gap, 0)`, with the gap to `target` as a share of the limit (`python/bots/template.py:723`). Off by default. On P3 round 1 and P4 rounds 1–2 it never beat 0, and price `skew` cost more (`IDEAS.md`).
