## The format swap, step by step

The one checklist for the swap; every other file points here. Everything it edits is prepared and registered: the plug-in `python/arena/formats/qminers.py` (a copy of `snapshots.py` with `# TODO(day)` markers and `SKELETON = True`), the bot `python/bots/qminers/template.py` (the template with PARAMS empty and its seven ADAPTER functions raising until written), the probe `python/bots/qminers/probe.py`, and `python/bots/qminers/template.py` as `--format qminers`'s default `--bot`. Copy neither `template.py` nor `drill/template.py`. Practised on the drill format (2026-10-17; `docs/TODO_DONE.md`, "Practise the format swap") and in the mock round (`IDEAS.md`, "The kickoff prompts on a disguised round"). `D` is `data/arena/round<N>/`.

1. **`formats/qminers.py`:** set `SKELETON = False`, then replace its two halves separately (its docstring says which lines are which):
   - **the loader:** for CSV snapshots, rename `FILES`, `COLUMNS`, `DELIMITER`, `DEPTH`, `PRICE_SCALE`; for an order-by-order log take `orderlog.py`'s loader, for book changes `drill.py`'s. Prices as integers of one unit for every product (the smallest their prices use), each product's tick and that unit in `Day.info[p] = {"tick", "unit"}` (prices, never credits), the credits a lot one unit of their quoted price is worth in `CREDITS` where it isn't 1 (the 2026-10-30 rehearsal's points were worth 2; calibrate and `--metrics` convert with it, and a fee or penalty given in credits goes into `ENGINE` divided by it), a limit per product in `Day.limits` (raise on a missing one), books best price first with positive volumes, the taker side kept (as the trade's buyer or seller), timestamps mapped onto 0..999,900 (step `n` of `N` as `n × 1,000,000 // N`, so `flatten_from` keeps its scale). The engine's convention for a tick's trades: the bot sees the previous tick's, and its orders meet this tick's (`run_market`), so a feed whose line `n` carries the trades since line `n − 1` puts line `n + 1`'s trades on tick `n`. A **level-indexed** book feed (rows by side and level number, a level's price changing in place, as the 2026-10-30 rehearsal's): keep each side as an array by level and rebuild the book from it each step (`python/experiments/mock_round/round3/results/after/qminers_format.py`'s loader), not `drill.py`'s price-keyed changes, which leave a moved level's old price standing; check in the rules or the first session whether the levels below an emptied one shift up.
2. **Check the loader before anything else:** `uv run python -m arena.diagnose all --format qminers --data D --profile --suggest`. Its spreads are in ticks; its prices (edge, R, drift, the fixed level) in the loader's unit; `--suggest`'s PARAMS in the bot's ticks where `Day.info` gives a tick.
3. **The wrapper half:** `ENTRY` and the bot class for a bot that answers a state with orders; a callback API with order ids and resting orders takes `drill.py`'s wrapper and `RESTING = True`. Whatever else their rules fix about matching goes in `ENGINE` (EngineConfig fields: `queue`, `trade_matching`, `fee`, `maker_fee`, `mark`, `limit_penalty`, the caps), so every tool runs under their rules with no flags; a flag still overrides a field for one run. Their order ids go on `Order.tag` (copied to the `OpenOrder` and `Fill`s); `begin_day(day)` gets `Day.info`; with `fresh_each_tick`, keep exactly the state their engine keeps between calls (the validator compares the two runs). `load_bot` must load the bot file before it returns (`arena.cli.bot_module` reads the module `BotFile.load` registers; otherwise `experiments.day.premise` fails with `KeyError: 'arena_bot_…'`; the 2026-10-28 rehearsal). Their caps go in `ENGINE` (`max_orders` new orders a call, `max_actions` orders and cancels, `max_resting` orders working a product), and the bot's ADAPTER passes the resting cap to `execute(..., max_resting=N)`, which drops the posts past the room rather than reposting. A rule the engine doesn't model (a limit check that rejects only the offending order, where resting mode rejects the tick's new orders for the product) goes in the wrapper, applied before the engine sees the orders. For a **function API** (module-level functions returning order dicts with the bot's own ids) the nearest wrapper is `python/experiments/mock_round/round3/results/after/qminers_format.py`'s, for a class API `python/experiments/mock_round/round2/results/before/qminers_format.py`'s. Provide exactly their API, nothing of ours: our Prosperity datamodel's extensions (`Cancel`, `ioc=True`, `state.open_orders`) would let a bot pass here and fail there.
4. **Their SDK or module file:** copied unchanged into `python/arena/formats/` and named in `PROVIDED_MODULES`, never retyped (a hand-written copy is how a field their engine rejects passes `arena.validate`; the 2026-10-26 audit, A3).
5. **`bots/qminers/template.py`:** rewrite only the imports, `PARAMS` (one entry per product from step 2's `--suggest`, prices in the product's ticks) and the seven ADAPTER functions plus `class Bot` (renamed to their entry). Prices become whole ticks at the boundary (STRATEGY's "one tick inside" is +1); resting orders oldest first; `limit` is the smaller of PARAMS' and the exchange's; the session's clock mapped onto 0..999,900 as in step 1. Three unit domains: the exchange's (the portal's credits), the engine's (the loader's unit: reports, `--metrics`, the stress table), and each product's ticks (`stop_loss` and every PARAMS price): the dry run's stops were off 2–10× before it converted. Where orders reach the exchange late (the rules, probe 3, or calibrate's `delay`), set the ADAPTER's `DELAY` to it and `ORDERS_REST` to whether orders rest: `on_the_way` and `went` then count what is sent and not yet listed against the limit and the resting cap, so the bot doesn't re-post it (both 2026-10-30 rehearsal uploads lost to that; the Arena's own order ids, if its API returns them, change nothing here). The ADAPTER for a function API with our own ids: `python/experiments/mock_round/round3/results/in_flight/template.py` (one action cap shared by the products, which take turns going first, and `DELAY` 1); for a class API, `python/experiments/mock_round/round2/results/before/template.py`.
6. **The sync and the tests:** `uv run python -m experiments.format_drill --sync`, then `uv run pytest python/tests/test_drill.py python/tests/test_skeletons.py python/tests/test_qminers_scaffold.py`.
7. **Validate:** `uv run python -m arena.validate python/bots/qminers/template.py all --format qminers --data D --time-limit-ms <theirs> --max-size <the dashboard's cap> [--allow numpy]`; over the cap, `arena.compact` and validate the copy. It also replays the first day with orders a step late and FAILs on refusals or breaches: set the bot's `DELAY` (step 5), or add `--late 0` only if their rules say orders arrive at once.
8. **The probe:** `python/bots/qminers/probe.py`'s import, `PRODUCT`, `LIMIT` and ADAPTER; `uv run python -m arena.backtest python/bots/qminers/probe.py all --format qminers --data D --print --no-strict`, then the playground.
9. **Every tool once on D:** backtest in each fill mode (after the first upload, `experiments.day.calibrate` fits the plug-in's `ENGINE` to its metrics, once step 11 has mapped the ZIP), grid, stress, analysis; a few fills compared with the playground by hand.
10. **The Rust twin, when grids get slow:** the template is a twin by config if its ADAPTER follows the drill's (or Prosperity's) conventions on six choices (prices in ticks or the unit, trader names, our fills among the trades, the limit's source, the products' order, resting orders sorted or as listed). Add the row to `rust/twins.json`: `{"kind": "template", "format": "qminers", "adapter": "drill" or {the six choices}, "rust": "rust/src/bots/template.rs", "reads": ["DEFAULTS", "PARAMS"], "sha256": ""}` (with `"max_resting": N` among the adapter's choices if its ADAPTER passes that cap), then `uv run python -m experiments.rust_bench --twin python/bots/qminers/template.py --data D --days all --format qminers --record` (32 cases, 6 base and 26 variants, on the round's own data; `rust/README.md`, "A new twin"). Any difference: grid in Python. The row's `exchange_limit` must match step 5's `min()`.
11. **The results ZIP:** rename `fill_curve.py`'s `RESULT_FILES` / `RESULT_COLUMNS` (and `SIDES`, `RESULT_PRICE_SCALE`, `RESULT_TIME_SCALE`, `DAY_NAMES`) at its top to the ZIP's names; then `experiments.day.fill_curve --results ZIP` and `experiments.day.shortfall --results ZIP` work.
12. **Rivals and `mm.py` have no ADAPTER** (`experiments.day.rival_field` refuses other formats): on their format `experiments.day.adversaries` and `experiments.day.quote_size` run only the template copy; `--vs` with copies of our bot works.
13. **Write it down:** the units map, the timestamp mapping, the limit's source and the fill model's fit into the round's `HANDOVER.md`; the rule answers under their "First hour" items.

## If no agent is available

1. **Format:** in `python/arena/formats/qminers.py`, rename the field names to theirs (step 1 of the swap above: the column or event names, `ENTRY`, `PRICE_SCALE`). Then `uv run python -m arena.diagnose all --format qminers --data D`: if it prints a table, the loader works.
2. **Bot:** in `python/bots/qminers/template.py`, write the seven ADAPTER functions from the drill bodies in their comments, and give every product a PARAMS entry from `arena.diagnose ... --suggest` (the first candidate to backtest and grid, not a tuned setting; `IDEAS.md`, "`arena.diagnose --suggest`").
3. **Check:** `uv run python -m arena.validate BOT all --format qminers --data D --time-limit-ms <theirs> --max-size <the dashboard's>`; fix every FAIL, then upload by the round's rule.
4. **If step 2 won't run in time:** a plain market maker straight against their API, about 40 lines: each step, for each product, fair = the mid of the largest bid and ask; cancel your resting orders; bid at best bid + 1 tick and ask at best ask − 1 tick, if still inside fair ± 1 tick, sized to the room left under the limit; take any ask below fair − 1 or bid above fair + 1. Seed nothing, read no clock.

- [ ] **Are there fees, rate limits or caps?** Fees or rebates per trade, the maximum number of orders per call or per second, and the maximum number of open orders. AlgoTrade closed your connection above 500 messages a second, and allowed 6,000 open orders.

## Probes

`python/bots/probe.py` (its copy for the day: `python/bots/qminers/probe.py`) answers most of the first-hour list in one playground run of about 420 ticks. It
runs probes 7, 1–2, 5, 3, 12, 4, 11, 17, 18, 13 and 9 in that order, each in its own window,
cancelling and flattening between them (except the lot probe 13 holds to the end). The three that can end the run come
last: 10 (importing numpy, pandas and scipy can be slow, and a sandbox that scans the source may refuse a file that uses
`importlib`), 6 (an exception) and 8 (sleeps up to 500 ms). They sit in one marked block, RISKY PROBES: if their rules
forbid dynamic imports or sleeps, or submissions are scarce, delete the block and upload the rest first. Each tick it prints a `SEEN` line with the book, position,
open orders and fills, and each probe prints one `PROBE <name>: <finding>` line. Rewrite its ADAPTER
for their API first, as for the template. In our own engine it finds:
- per tick: orders don't rest, and limits reject every order for the product;
- with `--resting`: orders rest, and the book doesn't show our own;
- both: no self-trade, fills reported one call later, called again after an exception, and every kind of state kept.

So any other answer on the platform is a difference to act on. Probes 14–16 compare with data outside the bot; 19–22 are not in `probe.py` yet. The probes:

1. **Resting:** at tick 1, bid well below the best bid. At tick 2, send nothing. If the order is still listed, or fills later, orders rest.
2. **Own orders in the book:** with that bid resting, check the book for a level at its price and size.
3. **Fill timing and price:** send a buy at the best ask. Is the fill reported in the same call, the next call, or later, and at which price? If the order traded against a later book than the one the bot saw (its price was gone, or it filled at a later price), the exchange applies orders late: backtest with `--delay 1` (or the delay measured), which on P3 round 1 halves the template's PnL (`IDEAS.md`, "Orders that arrive a tick late"), set the bot's `DELAY` (step 5 of the swap), and cap the quotes' size ("How are position limits enforced?")
4. **Limits:** send two buys that together exceed the limit by one. Is one rejected, both, or every order for the product? Is there an error message?
5. **Self-trade:** send a bid and an ask at the same price. Do they trade with each other?
6. **Exceptions:** raise an exception in one tick. Does the bot get called again?
7. **State:** set a module global and an instance attribute, then read them on the next call.
8. **Time limit:** sleep 50, 200 and 500 ms in different ticks. Note what happens at each.
9. **Prints:** print about 10 KB in one call. Where does it show up? Is it cut off?
10. **Imports:** inside `try`, import numpy, pandas and scipy, and print which ones loaded.
11. **Queue position:** join the best bid with a small order. Note the volume shown ahead of you, and how much trades at that price before you fill.
12. **Cancelling a filled order:** send a buy at the best ask, then cancel it by id on the next tick.
13. **What the score is marked to:** in the first tick buy one lot at the best ask and hold it. Compare the platform's PnL (or mark) over time with the mid, the volume-weighted mid and the wall-mid variants in `references/wiki/concepts/fair-value-and-wall-mid.md`. Linear Utility, Frankfurt and Baguette Fromage found the market maker's mid this way.
14. **Far quotes on an empty side:** when one side of the book is empty, quote a small order at increasing distances from the last fair value (for example 20, 50, 100). Note the distance at which it stops filling. Baguette Fromage and DU found hidden takers at about 100 (`references/wiki/concepts/hidden-takers-and-empty-sides.md`). Only this probe can test it: a replay fills a far quote only from a trade outside the book, and no Prosperity day has one (`experiments.day.structure --sections fingerprints`' last column on the download says whether ours do; `IDEAS.md`, "Quoting far away when one side of the book is empty").
15. **Is the practice data part of the scored data?** Compare the first rows of the public backtest data with the first ticks of an evaluated run (timestamps, prices, trade sizes). In Prosperity 3 the website's sample was the first 1,000 timestamps of live day 1. If `experiments.day.repeats` finds the download's takers recurring day to day ("Evaluation data"), also compare the evaluated run's trades with its predicted events: a match says the scored day replays the same schedule.
16. **Same trader, two oracles:** run one deterministic trader on the portal's public backtest and in our engine, and compare the fill lists. One team measured +2,415 locally against +436 live on the same window (`references/wiki/contradictions.md`, row 10). The portal's metrics (Known so far) are the quickest comparison: `uv run python -m arena.backtest <the uploaded file> <days> --format qminers --data <folder> --metrics` prints the same ones, per product and in total. Its two standard errors are guesses at the portal's (per-tick changes taken as independent, and 20 blocks' sums); whichever matches says how the portal computes it, and if neither does, the portal's PnL is probably an average over several runs ("How is PnL scored?").
17. **Own impact:** in a quiet product, take the whole best ask once, then send nothing for 20 ticks. Compare those ticks' books with the same timestamps in the downloaded data: identical books mean a replay; a moved wall or a slow refill means the market reacts to us.
18. **Passive fills and their markout:** for a few hundred ticks, keep one lot at the best bid and one at the best ask, with no signal, re-posting when the price moves away. Record how often each fills and the mid 1, 10 and 50 ticks after each fill. Negative markouts mean the fills come with adverse moves (`references/wiki/sources/negative-drift-of-a-fill.md`); run the same orders in our engine on the same data and compare (as in probe 16). This is the live experiment of `references/wiki/sources/market-makers-dilemma.md` in miniature. On Prosperity data the template's own passive fills had no state with a negative value (`IDEAS.md`, "Passive fills' value by state"): run `uv run python -m experiments.day.quote_markouts --data <folder> --days <days> --format qminers --bot <our bot>` on the download too, and if some state's total is negative on every day, gate that side in `make`.
19. **Hidden or reserve orders:** send an IOC buy for the displayed volume at the best ask plus one lot. A fill of the extra lot at that price means volume the book didn't show (Harris on reserve orders; AlgoTrade's ZSE printed 53.9% of its trades outside the shown top three levels, Known so far).
20. **A price band:** with one side full, send a small order far beyond it (say 20% from the mid). Rejected with a message means a band, and the message gives its width; resting means none, and probe 14's far quotes are possible.
21. **An opening auction:** read the first tick's trades in the download and the first upload's results. Many fills at one price in the first tick mean an auction: no quote of ours trades before it, and its price is a fair to start from.
22. **Other teams' orders in the feed:** look for trades or cancels in the state that no exchange bot and nothing of ours explains, and for an aggressor flag on trades. AlgoTrade sent every team's trades and cancels to everyone (Known so far). If ours does, markouts by aggressor, the spoof tell and reading rivals' archetypes work from the feed.

A feed with trades but no book still gives a spread: Roll's estimator, twice the square root of minus the covariance of consecutive price changes (`literature_md/harris-trading-and-exchanges/harris-trading-and-exchanges.md:9615-9668`).
