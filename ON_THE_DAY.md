## The format swap, step by step

Practised on the drill format (2026-10-17, `python/arena/formats/drill.py`, `python/bots/drill/template.py`), which differs from Prosperity's in every way we could think of. The plug-in took about 300 lines and the bot's ADAPTER about 100. Running the tools on it found four Prosperity habits, now fixed; the steps below avoid the rest.

1. **Copy the format nearest their shape to `qminers.py`** and register it in `FORMATS`. Set `RESTING = True` if their orders rest until cancelled. Three start points (`python/arena/formats/__init__.py`):
   - book snapshots in CSV, a bot answering a state with orders: `snapshots.py` (rename `FILES`, `COLUMNS`, `ENTRY`, set `PRICE_SCALE`; its `api` module is a stand-in for theirs);
   - an order-by-order log (orders added, changed, cancelled, executed by id): `orderlog.py` (rename `EVENTS`, `COLUMNS`), with the drill's bot API until theirs replaces it;
   - book changes or a callback API with order ids: `drill.py`, the one practised end to end.
   The skeletons load and run a bot (`python/tests/test_skeletons.py`), but no template has their ADAPTER: write it as `python/bots/drill/template.py`'s (step 5).
2. **The loader** fills `Day`, `Tick`, `Book` and `MarketTrade`:
   - Books best price first, with positive volumes. If they send book changes, keep the book and publish it once a step, as `drill.py` does.
   - **Prices as integers of one unit for every product**, the smallest their prices use (cents). Prices then compare exactly, and PnL adds up across products. Per-product tick sizes go in `Day.info`, limits in `Day.limits`, and the loader must raise on a product without one.
   - If trades say which side was the taker, keep it (drill: as the trade's buyer or seller).
3. **Check the loader before anything else:** `arena.diagnose` on their data. Its spreads are in ticks, but its prices (edge, R, drift, the fixed level) are in the loader's unit.
4. **The bot wrapper** builds their view each step and turns the bot's actions into `Order`s and `Cancel`s:
   - Their order ids map to the engine's through `Order.tag`. The engine copies the tag to the order's `OpenOrder` and `Fill`s.
   - `begin_day(day)` gives the wrapper `Day.info`.
   - With `fresh_each_tick`, keep exactly the state their engine keeps between calls, and drop the rest (the validator compares the two runs).
   - Provide the bot exactly their API: their classes, their constructor signatures, nothing of ours. `arena.validate` can only catch a call their exchange would reject if the provided module rejects it too. Our Prosperity datamodel has extensions (`Cancel`, `Order(..., ioc=True)`, `state.open_orders`), so a bot that passes against it could fail on IMC's real one.
   - **If they hand out a module or SDK file, the plug-in provides that file unchanged:** copy it into `python/arena/formats/` as it came and list it in `PROVIDED_MODULES`; never retype it. Whatever our engine needs goes in the wrapper around it. A hand-written copy is how a field name, a type or a keyword argument their engine rejects passes `arena.validate` and fails every tick on theirs (the 2026-10-26 audit, A3).
5. **The bot:** in a copy of `python/bots/template.py`, rewrite the imports, PARAMS and ADAPTER, and nothing else. `python/bots/drill/template.py` shows how:
   - Convert their prices to whole ticks of each product at the boundary, and back. STRATEGY's "one tick inside" means +1.
   - Pass resting orders oldest first.
   - Take the limit from their API if it gives one, but let PARAMS ask for less (the smaller of the two), so `limit` can be gridded; the drill ADAPTER overrides it silently (the 2026-10-26 dry run).
   - Map a session onto the template's timestamps 0..999,900 (the dry run's ADAPTER sent step × 100), so `flatten_from`, the day-length settings and the stress perturbations keep their Prosperity scale.
6. **Run every tool once on their data** (`backtest`, `validate`, `grid`, `stress`, `analysis`), then compare a few fills by hand with their example or the playground.

## If no agent is available

For an hour of an outage or a rate limit (the second audit, A10). Everything below runs without an agent; the second laptop keeps ChatGPT logged in (we have both ChatGPT and Anthropic accounts, the user, 2026-10-26), and `KICKOFF_PROMPT.md`'s prompt without the repository works with any agent.
1. **Format:** copy the nearest skeleton (step 1 of the swap above) and rename its field names to theirs: the column or event names, the entry point, `PRICE_SCALE`. Then `uv run python -m arena.diagnose all --format qminers --data D`: if it prints a table, the loader works.
2. **Bot:** copy `python/bots/drill/template.py` to `python/bots/qminers/template.py` and rewrite only its imports and ADAPTER (`read_book`, `read_trades`, `read_fills`, `params_for`, the entry class) to their API; give every product a PARAMS entry (`fair: "sized_mid"`, the limit) from diagnose. `arena.diagnose ... --suggest` prints one per product from the notes (prices in each product's ticks where the format gives them): the first candidate to backtest and grid, not a tuned setting. On P2–P4's held-out days it matched the hand-tuned market makers (+0.4%) and fell short where a level is faded (56% of it: the band needs a grid), and on ABIDES came out about level with the template as is (`IDEAS.md`, "`arena.diagnose --suggest`").
3. **Check:** `uv run python -m arena.validate BOT all --format qminers --data D --time-limit-ms <theirs> --max-size <the dashboard's>`; fix every FAIL, then upload by the round's rule.
4. **If step 2 won't run in time:** a plain market maker straight against their API, about 40 lines: each step, for each product, fair = the mid of the largest bid and ask; cancel your resting orders; bid at best bid + 1 tick and ask at best ask − 1 tick, if still inside fair ± 1 tick, sized to the room left under the limit; take any ask below fair − 1 or bid above fair + 1. Seed nothing, read no clock.

Whichever finds the cell, the gates and `--holdout` decide whether to use it: on Prosperity the training best was often not the test best.

## Probes

`python/bots/probe.py` answers most of the first-hour list in one playground run of about 420 ticks. It
runs probes 7, 1–2, 5, 3, 12, 4, 11, 17, 18, 13 and 9 in that order, each in its own window,
cancelling and flattening between them (except the lot probe 13 holds to the end). The three that can end the run come
last: 10 (importing numpy, pandas and scipy can be slow, and a sandbox that scans the source may refuse a file that uses
`importlib`), 6 (an exception) and 8 (sleeps up to 500 ms). They sit in one marked block, RISKY PROBES: if their rules
forbid dynamic imports or sleeps, or submissions are scarce, delete the block and upload the rest first. Each tick it prints a `SEEN` line with the book, position,
open orders and fills, and each probe prints one `PROBE <name>: <finding>` line. Rewrite its ADAPTER
for their API first, as for the template. In our own engine it finds:
- per tick: orders don't rest, and limits reject every order for the product;
- with `--resting`: orders rest, and the book doesn't show our own;
- both: no self-trade, fills reported one call later, called again after an exception, and every kind of state kept.

So any other answer on the platform is a difference to act on. Probes 14–16 compare with data outside the bot; 19–22 are not in `probe.py` yet. The probes:

1. **Resting:** at tick 1, bid well below the best bid. At tick 2, send nothing. If the order is still listed, or fills later, orders rest.
2. **Own orders in the book:** with that bid resting, check the book for a level at its price and size.
3. **Fill timing and price:** send a buy at the best ask. Is the fill reported in the same call, the next call, or later, and at which price? If the order traded against a later book than the one the bot saw (its price was gone, or it filled at a later price), the exchange applies orders late: backtest with `--delay 1` (or the delay measured), which on P3 round 1 halves the template's PnL (`IDEAS.md`, "Orders that arrive a tick late"), and cap the quotes' size ("How are position limits enforced?")
4. **Limits:** send two buys that together exceed the limit by one. Is one rejected, both, or every order for the product? Is there an error message?
5. **Self-trade:** send a bid and an ask at the same price. Do they trade with each other?
6. **Exceptions:** raise an exception in one tick. Does the bot get called again?
7. **State:** set a module global and an instance attribute, then read them on the next call.
8. **Time limit:** sleep 50, 200 and 500 ms in different ticks. Note what happens at each.
9. **Prints:** print about 10 KB in one call. Where does it show up? Is it cut off?
10. **Imports:** inside `try`, import numpy, pandas and scipy, and print which ones loaded.
11. **Queue position:** join the best bid with a small order. Note the volume shown ahead of you, and how much trades at that price before you fill.
12. **Cancelling a filled order:** send a buy at the best ask, then cancel it by id on the next tick.
13. **What the score is marked to:** in the first tick buy one lot at the best ask and hold it. Compare the platform's PnL (or mark) over time with the mid, the volume-weighted mid and the wall-mid variants in `references/wiki/concepts/fair-value-and-wall-mid.md`. Linear Utility, Frankfurt and Baguette Fromage found the market maker's mid this way.
14. **Far quotes on an empty side:** when one side of the book is empty, quote a small order at increasing distances from the last fair value (for example 20, 50, 100). Note the distance at which it stops filling. Baguette Fromage and DU found hidden takers at about 100 (`references/wiki/concepts/hidden-takers-and-empty-sides.md`).
15. **Is the practice data part of the scored data?** Compare the first rows of the public backtest data with the first ticks of an evaluated run (timestamps, prices, trade sizes). In Prosperity 3 the website's sample was the first 1,000 timestamps of live day 1. If `experiments.repeats` finds the download's takers recurring day to day ("Evaluation data"), also compare the evaluated run's trades with its predicted events: a match says the scored day replays the same schedule.
16. **Same trader, two oracles:** run one deterministic trader on the portal's public backtest and in our engine, and compare the fill lists. One team measured +2,415 locally against +436 live on the same window (`references/wiki/contradictions.md`, row 10). The portal's metrics (Known so far) are the quickest comparison: `uv run python -m arena.backtest <the uploaded file> <days> --format qminers --data <folder> --metrics` prints the same ones, per product and in total. Its two standard errors are guesses at the portal's (per-tick changes taken as independent, and 20 blocks' sums); whichever matches says how the portal computes it, and if neither does, the portal's PnL is probably an average over several runs ("How is PnL scored?").
17. **Own impact:** in a quiet product, take the whole best ask once, then send nothing for 20 ticks. Compare those ticks' books with the same timestamps in the downloaded data: identical books mean a replay; a moved wall or a slow refill means the market reacts to us.
18. **Passive fills and their markout:** for a few hundred ticks, keep one lot at the best bid and one at the best ask, with no signal, re-posting when the price moves away. Record how often each fills and the mid 1, 10 and 50 ticks after each fill. Negative markouts mean the fills come with adverse moves (`references/wiki/sources/negative-drift-of-a-fill.md`); run the same orders in our engine on the same data and compare (as in probe 16). This is the live experiment of `references/wiki/sources/market-makers-dilemma.md` in miniature. On Prosperity data the template's own passive fills had no state with a negative value (`IDEAS.md`, "Passive fills' value by state"): run `uv run python -m experiments.quote_markouts --data <folder> --days <days> --format qminers --bot <our bot>` on the download too, and if some state's total is negative on every day, gate that side in `make`.
19. **Hidden or reserve orders:** send an IOC buy for the displayed volume at the best ask plus one lot. A fill of the extra lot at that price means volume the book didn't show (Harris on reserve orders; AlgoTrade's ZSE printed 53.9% of its trades outside the shown top three levels, Known so far).
20. **A price band:** with one side full, send a small order far beyond it (say 20% from the mid). Rejected with a message means a band, and the message gives its width; resting means none, and probe 14's far quotes are possible.
21. **An opening auction:** read the first tick's trades in the download and the first upload's results. Many fills at one price in the first tick mean an auction: no quote of ours trades before it, and its price is a fair to start from.
22. **Other teams' orders in the feed:** look for trades or cancels in the state that no exchange bot and nothing of ours explains, and for an aggressor flag on trades. AlgoTrade sent every team's trades and cancels to everyone (Known so far). If ours does, markouts by aggressor, the spoof tell and reading rivals' archetypes work from the feed.

A feed with trades but no book still gives a spread: Roll's estimator, twice the square root of minus the covariance of consecutive price changes (`literature_md/harris-trading-and-exchanges/harris-trading-and-exchanges.md:9615-9668`).
