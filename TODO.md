## Decisions

Choices only the user can make, each with the options, the evidence and a recommendation.

- [ ] **Should new products default to `sized_mid`?** `DEFAULTS["fair"]` is `"mid"`, so a product not tuned yet quotes around the noisiest price (Kelp's mid varies 28 times more per tick at lag 1 than at lag 200). The Prosperity products set `wall_mid` in their PARAMS, which is best when trading alone and is what the recorded benchmarks used.
  - Evidence (`IDEAS.md`, "Wall-mid definitions"): trading alone, `sized_mid` is within 2.3% of the best definition on every product tested. With the teams' P4 bots resting orders in the same market, `wall_mid` lost 377k / 445k on Osmium in rounds 1 / 2, and `sized_mid` made +39k / +21k.
  - Options: (a) keep `"mid"`; (b) `DEFAULTS["fair"] = "sized_mid"` and leave the Prosperity PARAMS on `wall_mid`; (c) also move the Prosperity PARAMS to `sized_mid`, giving up as much as 2.3% alone and re-recording the benchmarks.
  - Recommendation: (b). It costs nothing on the products we have tuned. On the day every product starts untuned, before we know whether other teams' orders rest in the same book, and there `sized_mid` is both far less noisy than the mid (signature ratio 3.7 against 29.6 on Kelp, fair-value page) and the only definition that held up against resting rivals. `wall_size` (15) then needs checking against the market maker's usual size on the day (`ON_THE_DAY.md`, "Is `wall_mid` still the market maker's price?").
  - Done when: the user has chosen, and `DEFAULTS`, `README.md` and `ON_THE_DAY.md` agree with the choice.

- [ ] **Should Hydrogel move to the setting that's best under the fixed `level_exit`?** The committed `level_band` 30, `clear_edge` 8, `level_exit` 0 was tuned while `level_exit` only fired at a gap of exactly 0. After the fix (`9f13090`), Hydrogel's in-sample PnL fell from 165k to 156k.
  - Evidence (`IDEAS.md`, "Trading toward a known level"): re-gridded in-sample on P4 rounds 3–4, `level_band` 40, `clear_edge` 10, `level_exit` 0 is best on every measure (pnl, nbhd, worst day, `worse` fills). It is only 7.8k ahead (1,165k against 1,157k for the whole bot, 0.7%), and the committed setting ranks third. 40 and 10 are at the edge of the grid's band and `clear_edge` axes. The held-out days chose 40 / 10 / 0 in three folds of four.
  - Options: (a) keep 30 / 8 / 0; (b) switch to 40 / 10 / 0; (c) widen the grid (band up to 60, `clear_edge` up to 14) first and pick from an interior optimum.
  - Recommendation: (c), then (b) only if 40 / 10 isn't on the new edge too. The gain is too small to act on while the best cell sits on the grid's boundary. Hydrogel is a Prosperity product, so this only matters as a benchmark, not for the Arena.
  - Done when: the user has chosen, and `PARAMS` and the `IDEAS.md` row agree with the choice.
- [ ] **When the bid and ask meet at target, which one steps back?** Since `9f13090`, `make` keeps the bid below the ask. If they meet, it moves the side that would add to inventory: the bid when the position is at or above target, the ask when below. Exactly at target neither adds, and the tie goes to stepping the bid back, which leans slightly toward selling.
  - Evidence: it only happens with a narrow book and `make_edge` 0, or with a skew. No benchmark moved when the rule went in (P3 round 1: 81,292 unchanged; the Kelp `make_edge=0` grid row went from 63,068 to 63,054 under `worse`).
  - Options: (a) keep the tie on the bid; (b) at target, step back the side whose last fill was more recent, so neither side is favoured over a day; (c) at target, quote neither side that tick.
  - Recommendation: (a) for now: nothing measurable rides on it. Revisit if a product on the day is quoted at `make_edge` 0. (When they meet, both quotes sit at fair, so "the side further from fair" can't break the tie.)
  - Done when: the user has chosen, and `make`'s docstring and its test agree with the choice.

- [ ] **Add an optimistic queue option to `--resting`.** Today a new order always joins the back of the queue. Prosperity's bots re-quote every tick, so a real resting order there would often be at the front, and the truth probably lies between the two. Model both and backtest under each.

For the middle, copy its `ProbQueueModel` (`queue.rs`): when the visible level shrinks by `chg` (trades already removed), the volume ahead of you shrinks by `(1 - p) * chg`, where `p = f(back) / (f(back) + f(front))` is the probability that the decrease was *behind* you (the source's docstring says so; Rigtorp states the same thing from the other side). On Prosperity's wide spreads the papers predict a small `back` to `front` gap. See `references/wiki/concepts/queue-position-and-fills.md`.

## Strategy

- [ ] **Explain Kelp's long drift.** Passive buys exceed passive sells (3,629 against 2,330 over round 1), Kelp sits at +50 about 10% of the time, and `skew` doesn't help. Find out whether fair value or quoting is biased, using the fill tables and product charts.
  - **Two tests from the literature (2026-10-12):** (1) Harris: if counterparties are informed, our inventory is *negatively* correlated with the next price changes. Compute corr(`position`, future change in `mark`) for Kelp per day. (2) Cartea et al.: when one side's market orders arrive more often, the mid drifts and a maker without skew accumulates the side being hit. Compare the fill rates of our bids and asks with the drift of `mark`. See `references/wiki/concepts/informed-counterparties.md`.
  - Done when: the cause is known, and the fix (or the reason not to fix it) is in the template or in a note here.
- [ ] **Try the literature's fair values and skew in the template.** Candidates: the micro-price, order-book imbalance (Cont et al.; best-level volume imbalance `ρ = (Vᵇ − Vᵃ)/(Vᵇ + Vᵃ)` from Cartea, Donnelly & Jaimungal 2018, `literature_md/order-book-signals/`), heyman7913's blend `0.30 × volume-weighted mid + 0.70 × slow EMA + 2.0 × ρ` (`references/wiki/sources/prosperity4-heyman7913.md`), and Avellaneda–Stoikov's inventory skew in place of our linear one. Compare them with `wall_mid` under `worse` fills, `--resting` and `--vs`.
  - **Prior from Prosperity 4:** teams disagree about inventory skew on a pegged product. DTU Quant Lab lost 16,593 in backtest with an Avellaneda–Stoikov reservation price on Osmium against a fixed fair value with a micro-price refinement, while Baguette Fromage shipped a linear reserve-price shift (γ = 1/12) there. - [x] **The template's error handler can itself raise.** Done 2026-10-13: `open_orders(state)` reads the extension through `getattr`, the handler's own cancels sit in a `try`, and `arena.validate` replays the first day with `STRICT` off. `read_resting` and the `except` branch of `Trader.run` read `state.open_orders` directly.
