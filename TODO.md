- [ ] **Add an optimistic queue option to `--resting`.** Today a new order always joins the back of the queue. Prosperity's bots re-quote every tick, so a real resting order there would often be at the front, and the truth probably lies between the two. Model both and backtest under each.
  - **Our model is already hftbacktest's `RiskAdverseQueueModel`** (checked against `queue.rs`, see `references/README.md`), so the pessimistic end is a known quantity. For the middle, copy its `ProbQueueModel` (`queue.rs`): when the visible level shrinks by `chg` (trades already removed), the volume ahead of you shrinks by `(1 - p) * chg`, where `p = f(back) / (f(back) + f(front))` is the probability that the decrease was *behind* you (the source's docstring says so; Rigtorp states the same thing from the other side). Prefer `f(x) = log(1 + x)` to `x**n`: it shifts blame toward the back (front 100, back 10: 9% behind with `f(x) = x`, 34% with `log(1 + x)`), which is the direction the evidence points. Rigtorp's article and Databento's comparison against order-by-order data (both read, see `references/SEARCH_LOG.md`) find that real cancels come mostly from the back of the queue, and Databento warns that models assuming too little of that bias overstate passive fills. A front-of-queue mode is the other end. Read `literature_md/queue-position-valuation/` first (Moallemi & Yuan) for why position in the queue has a value.
  - **From the literature (2026-10-12):** Guo et al.'s `Υ` gives the general form, the share of cancellations falling ahead of you. Ours is `Υ ≡ 0`, `f(x) = x` is uniform cancellation (Moallemi & Yuan's assumption, which Huang et al. say overstates fills), and `log(1 + x)` sits between. On Prosperity's wide spreads the papers predict a small `back` to `front` gap. See `references/wiki/concepts/queue-position-and-fills.md`.
