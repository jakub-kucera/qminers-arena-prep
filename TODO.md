- [ ] **Second pass on overfitting: the methods beyond Bailey and López de Prado.** Raised 2026-10-19, ahead of the decision "Rewrite the engine and the strategies in a fast language for huge sweeps?". The first pass covered the three Bailey/López de Prado papers (MinBTL, PBO by CSCV, DSR, all in `arena.grid`) and the Prosperity teams' practice (`references/wiki/concepts/validation-and-overfitting.md`). Not read yet (citations from memory, check each one before ingesting it):
  - **Bootstrap tests of the best of N against a benchmark:** White's Reality Check (Econometrica 2000), Hansen's SPA test (JBES 2005), Romano & Wolf's stepwise test (Econometrica 2005), with the stationary bootstrap (Politis & Romano 1994) for dependent PnL. They are alternatives to the DSR that use the trials' joint distribution, not one `N̂`.
  - **Purging and embargo, and combinatorial purged CV** (López de Prado, *Advances in Financial Machine Learning*, ch. 7 and 12; Arian, Norouzi & Seco 2024, comparing CPCV with walk-forward on synthetic data). Our CSCV blocks touch, and positions and autocorrelation carry across a block boundary.
  - **Haircut Sharpe ratios and false discovery rates for many signals** (Harvey & Liu, "Backtesting", 2015; Harvey, Liu & Zhu 2016, the t > 3 hurdle).
  - **Reusing a holdout adaptively** (Dwork et al., "The reusable holdout", Science 2015). This is the theory for capped portal submissions and for "every look is a trial".
  - **Permutation tests of the whole search** (Masters, *Testing and Tuning Market Trading Systems*, 2018): rerun the entire grid on data with its structure destroyed, and see how much "edge" the search itself finds.
  - **Trading rules from a calibrated process instead of a backtest search** (Carr & López de Prado 2014, "Determining optimal trading rules without backtesting"). This is the theory behind searching `arena.sim` days.
  - **Choosing a plateau or an average of settings rather than the argmax.** `nbhd` does the first, nothing does the second.
  - Done when: each is found and either saved (or listed in `MANUAL_TODO.md` if blocked), converted and given a source page, or ticked as not needed; `validation-and-overfitting.md` says which method would change a choice `arena.grid` makes on a few days of tick data, and any worth building is its own `TODO.md` item; the lint passes.

## Strategy

- [ ] **Explain Kelp's long drift.** Passive buys exceed passive sells (3,629 against 2,330 over round 1), Kelp sits at +50 about 10% of the time, and `skew` doesn't help. Find out whether fair value or quoting is biased, using the fill tables and product charts.
  - **Two tests from the literature (2026-10-12):** (1) Harris: if counterparties are informed, our inventory is *negatively* correlated with the next price changes. Compute corr(`position`, future change in `mark`) for Kelp per day. (2) Cartea et al.: when one side's market orders arrive more often, the mid drifts and a maker without skew accumulates the side being hit. Compare the fill rates of our bids and asks with the drift of `mark`. See `references/wiki/concepts/informed-counterparties.md`.
  - Done when: the cause is known, and the fix (or the reason not to fix it) is in the template or in a note here.
  - **Part of the cause (2026-10-18): the takers are one-sided.** On P3 round 1, 35% of Kelp's trades at the touch are buys (42% of the volume: the buys are larger), so a two-sided quote is hit on the bid more often. In `arena.sim`'s `KELP_FLOW` preset, which copies that flow onto Kelp, the template's passive buys outnumber its sells 1.37 to 1 (P3 replay: 1.28; balanced `KELP`: 0.97), and its mean position is +5.1 (P3 replay: +24.5, `KELP`: −0.7). So the flow explains the direction and the fill imbalance, but only a fifth of the position; the rest is still open.
